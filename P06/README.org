Lecture 06 put models on the Princeton window that had never seen a Princeton label: a segment-anything model that cuts a mask from a click or a word, a model that compares pictures with captions, a model that answers questions about a picture, and a planetary model that wrote 64 numbers for every 10 m cell of the town. These four notebooks put those models and their numbers on your laptop. They follow the lecture's four chapters, and each opens on the whole product before it works on one block, one set of chips or one click. You run and read. You do not write code. None of it is assessed.

Start with =06_01=. Its first cell prints the window, whether the deep learning library is ready, and whether the open models can be loaded; if that works your environment is ready for the rest. Then read in order. Every number a notebook computes is printed beside the number the course pipeline computed from the same files, which travel in =data/ground_truth.json=, so you can see at once whether your run agrees.

Every computation is one call into =fm_tools.py=, the kit's toolbox. It holds the loaders of the two open models, the prompts, the metrics of Precept 05, the parsing of a vision-language model's answers, the three operations on an embedding and the plots, written with a sentence of explanation on each, and you can open it and read how any step works. The course pipeline that computes the reference numbers imports the same file, so the notebooks and the references cannot disagree.

The cells that run a model sit under =if torch_ok and ready[...]:= and print one line when the deep learning library, the transformers library or the weights are missing, so every notebook runs to its end either way, and the shipped masks and numbers carry what a skipped cell would have computed. The transformers library is in the course environment from this week on, and the container carries the weights of SAM 2.1 hiera-tiny (156 MB) and CLIP ViT-B/16 (600 MB) in its cache; pull and rebuild the container once before you start. On the uv path the weights download once, about 750 MB, the first time a loader runs. The vision-language model is not cached; its live cell sits behind =LIVE = False= and the kit ships its answers.

Each notebook ends with "Your turn": a cell that runs as it is, with one marked value to change. Change it, run again, and answer one question in words. The answers come in the Friday post, not in the kit. "Check your understanding" asks four questions you can answer from what the notebook showed. The =my_images= folder is for your own pictures; its README says what happens to them.

The data in =data/= is the lecture's running example cut to what a laptop needs. From the NAIP 2023 window of Lecture 05: the window at 2.4 m, the five test blocks at 30 cm with their masks, the footprints that touch them and the fine-tuned U-Net's maps, the masks of the four validation blocks, and the table of the 1,600 chips. From the lecture's runs: the instance masks of SAM 3 under building, house and roof on the nine held-out blocks and under three more words on block 7; the open stand-in's masks on the same blocks; the crop of block 7 with the lecture's prompts, the masks SAM 2.1 hiera-large gave them, its 171 everything masks and CLIP's name for each; CLIP's 512 numbers of every chip with four caption pairs; and the answers of Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct and SmolVLM-500M-Instruct to the class question on 320 chips, the count on 20 and a caption on four. From the embeddings: the AlphaEarth vectors of the town for 2021 and 2025 at 10 m on the Lecture 04 grid, cells outside the municipal boundary set to nodata; the cosine of every cell's 2018 and 2025 vectors as a stability raster; WorldCover 2021 collapsed to the four classes of Lecture 04 with its reference cells, its balanced pool, the held-out blocks of the lecture's curve, the forest's map and the lecture's own cluster maps. And the toy, its seven places as pictures, and one photograph of the class flight for the own-image lane. =data/scene.json= records the window, the blocks, the prompts, the words and captions, the places, the lecture's numbers and the choices the pipeline made.
