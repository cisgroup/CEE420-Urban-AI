The same field from orbit, from an aeroplane and, on the field day, from a drone we fly ourselves. Precept 03 is a field day, so nothing in this folder is run together in class. Every notebook here is self-paced, finished, and shipped with its outputs, so you can read it before you run it. None of it is assessed. All eight are here from the start. The five above the line use public imagery of the site and the photographs of the pre-flight of 17 September. The three below it read the products the software made from those photographs, the orthomosaic, the surface model and the point cloud, and they are re-run unchanged on the class flight once it has been processed, so what changes between the two versions is the data and the numbers, not a word of the prose.

Start with the first cells of =03_01=. They open five files of the same lawn and print a table of three coordinate systems, one of them in US survey feet, and if that works your environment is ready for the rest of this kit and for P04 on 30 September. Then read in order. Every notebook prints the numbers it computes beside the numbers the lecture quoted, which travel in =data/ground_truth.json=, so nothing is typed twice.

03_05 and the notebooks that follow read a flight's photographs and the products the software builds from them, and they check the result against the federal and the State's photographs of the same ground. They were built and run on the pre-flight of the same field first and are re-run on the class flight with the same code and prose, so what changes between the two versions is the data folder and the numbers in =data/ground_truth.json=; =data/flight.json= names the flight a notebook was last run on, and where a number belongs to one particular flight the prose says so by name.

The data in =data/= is small pinned windows of public imagery around Poe Field, the site of the flight, cut from the same files the lecture's figures were drawn from: a Landsat 9 pass, two Sentinel-2 passes over the 4 km campus window, two NAIP flights, the State's 2020 orthophoto at one foot, the USGS 3DEP terrain model at one metre, the outline of the field with the five sample places and the buildings around it from OpenStreetMap, and the pre-flight of 17 September 2026: a table of every photograph's metadata, four downsized photographs, the orthomosaic around the field at 10 cm with one pinned crop at its full resolution, the surface and terrain models, a sample of the point cloud, the software's report and the registration checks, all the course's own work under CC BY 4.0. The full products are too large for a repository and are not published; they stay with the instructor, =data/flight.json= records their size and shape, and the last section of 03_06 shows how a window of a file that size is read when it does live at an address. Every raster carries its source, its licence and its retrieval date in its tags, and every vector layer in its metadata. Sources and licences are listed in =data/README.org=. The Sentinel-2 windows contain modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2025; the OpenStreetMap layers are ODbL 1.0, copyright OpenStreetMap contributors. Nothing that identifies a person is in any file.
