Lecture 07 read Princeton as a graph of streets with places on it and put people on it: three networks from OpenStreetMap with their shortest paths, centralities and compass roses; isochrones, the two accessibility measures and the 15-minute city on the 839 cells of the P02 grid; one visitor's day of GPS fixes turned into stays and trips by one rule and scored against the diary that coded the day; a city's origin-destination matrix with two classical models of flows; and the privacy of location data, from the few points that identify a person to the counts that protect them. These four notebooks put all of it on your laptop, and the third runs your own trace of the scavenger hunt through the same chain. You run and read. You do not write code. None of it is assessed.

Start with =07_01=. Its first cell prints the networks' snapshot date and whether the class's counts are published; if that works your environment is ready for the rest. Then read in order. Every number a notebook computes is printed beside the number the course pipeline computed from the same files, which travel in =data/ground_truth.json=, so you can see at once whether your run agrees.

Every computation is one call into =mob_tools.py=, the kit's toolbox, which the lecture's figures import too. It holds the loaders, the shortest paths, isochrones and centralities, the two accessibility measures, the stay rule and its scoring, the origin-destination matrices with the gravity and radiation models, the uniqueness count, aggregation and noise, the readers for your own trace and the plots, written with a sentence of explanation on each, and you can open it and read how any step works. The course pipeline that computes the reference numbers imports the same file, so the notebooks and the references cannot disagree.

Each notebook ends with "Your turn": a cell that runs as it is, with one marked value to change. Change it, run again, and answer one question in words. The answers come in the Friday post, not in the kit. "Check your understanding" asks four questions you can answer from what the notebook showed. The =my_traces= folder is for your own trace of the hunt and the diary your team kept; its README says what happens to them. The class's traces reach the kit, if they do, only as counts of trackers per cell and hour above a minimum count, in =data/class_counts.csv.gz=, and the fourth notebook says so when they are not there yet.

The data in =data/= is the lecture's running example cut to what a laptop needs. From OpenStreetMap through osmnx 2.1 on 30 October 2026: the drive, bike and walk networks of the municipality and the drive networks of Trenton and Hopewell Borough, projected to EPSG:26918 with speeds, travel times and bearings; the 293 destinations of six kinds; the lecture's isochrones from Nassau Hall and the duel at E225. From the course: the municipal boundary, the 839 cells of the P02 grid with the census block groups' residents spread over them by built area, the lecture's table of the cells with the minutes its routing run produced over the NJ Transit schedule, every cell's minutes to the stop at Princeton Junction on foot, by transit and by car, the Dinky's timetable with the router's minutes from the cell of Nassau Hall, and the walk betweenness, exact from all 6,814 nodes. From the theme-park study of Utley and Hackl 2026: visitor KP2's fixes, the fifteen Knott's Berry Farm visitors' fixes, their coded stops and summary rows, the park's outline, paths and points of interest, and the lecture's threshold surface. From Porto's taxis: the origin-destination counts on cells of 500 m, 1 km and 2 km, the cell and hour of every taxi-day's departures and arrivals, and the trip-length histogram. From the Census Bureau: the LODES 2023 flows between the tracts within 40 km with the tracts' residents and jobs, Princeton's in-commuters by home tract in New Jersey and Pennsylvania, the block pairs of the town's jobs, and the ACS 2020 to 2024 estimates of the 21 block groups. =data/scene.json= records the rules, the anchors, the lecture's numbers, the choices the pipeline made and the provenance of every file.
